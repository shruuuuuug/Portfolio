My Background / About Me
I'm a junior at UW-Madison studying Environmental Science and Computer Science, driven by a long-standing passion for both conservation and technology. Growing up, I was equally drawn to the outdoors and to computers; I spent time exploring my local forests while also learning how to build computers from scratch. That dual interest naturally evolved into an interest in using data/computation to better understand and protect ecosystems.
My studies delve into on-field ecological research with data analysis. I've excavated soil pits, conducted wet-aggregate stability and mean weight diameter soil tests, analyzed nutrient cycling, and studied how wildlife influences forest soil dynamics. In earlier research, I investigated Pythium species under controlled conditions to understand their thermal and nutritional responses, and I contributed to projects examining coal plant retirement strategies and climate resilience.
I'm motivated by big ecological questions: how ecosystems respond to climate change, how soil processes regulate nutrient cycling, and how data-driven methods can inform more effective conservation strategies. I hope to continue bridging ecology, technology, and sustainability to support resilient ecosystems and conservation solutions.
